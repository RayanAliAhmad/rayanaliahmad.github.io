"Film as dream, film as music. No art passes our conscience in the way film does, and goes directly to our feelings, deep down into the dark rooms of our souls."— Ingmar Bergman

I'm not interested in the moment someone performs for a lens. I'm interested in the one after, when the guard drops, and what's left is just a face, doing what faces do when no one's watching: aging, doubting, holding something back. That's the only frame worth keeping.

Beirut is a difficult place to photograph honestly, because it never stops performing either, for tourists, for the news, for itself. So I wait. I wait for the performance to end and the truth to walk in uninvited.

I'm Rayan A. Ali-Ahmad. I've been doing this for six years, quietly, in rooms most cameras don't enter. The work has been seen, exhibited internationally, published in the Boston Review, watched by more people than I'll ever meet, but none of that is the point. The point is the silence right before someone tells you who they really are.

If that's the kind of story you're carrying, bring it to me.
